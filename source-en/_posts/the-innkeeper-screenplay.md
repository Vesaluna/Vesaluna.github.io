A reading version of my WebGAL script 『The Innkeeper』, arranged in three acts while retaining the original sequence and dialogue. Game instructions for waiting, character sprites and transitions have been removed. This is an alternate-future setting: the characters are adults.

<div class="screenplay">

<p class="script-act" id="act-1">Act One</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Soyo Nagasaki and Uika Misumi face each other in Sakiko Togawa's inn.)</p>

<p>Uika: You and I are quite different.</p>

<p>Soyo: Here, my money spends just as well as yours.</p>

<p>Uika: If Saki gives me special attention, I deserve it.</p>

<p>Soyo: Oh? On what grounds?</p>

<p>Uika: I have a doctorate in mathematics from Queenston.</p>

<p>Soyo: What a coincidence. I have a master's degree.</p>

<p>Uika: A worthless master's you bought yourself.</p>

<p>Soyo: Yes… bought at that very university you're so proud of.</p>

<p>Uika: Enough! Show me some respect.</p>

<p>Soyo: Who's disrespecting you? Can you find anyone more impertinent here?</p>

<p>Uika: You know why I'm here. I've made my claim clear to everyone. Stop staring at her.</p>

<p>Soyo: You want to stop me? What do you think I visit this place every day for?</p>

<p>Uika: Whatever. You don't matter.</p>

<p>Soyo: I don't? And you do?</p>

<p>Uika: I am who I am… Sakiko needs my protection.</p>

<p>Soyo: Saki needs money, not your protection.</p>

<p>Uika: Money? She has enough.</p>

<p>Soyo: Dr Misumi, I spend ten thousand yen here every day. I've lost count of the presents I've given her.</p>

<p>Uika: She knows what I've done without my telling her.</p>

<p>Soyo: Of course. You may not say it, but everyone knows.</p>

<p>Uika: What could they possibly know?</p>

<p>Soyo: Well… a waiter told me you only spend five hundred yen a day on a glass of water. How thrifty.</p>

<p>Uika: … Speaking of waiters, that Mutsumi Wakaba is the most disagreeable. Sakiko is inexplicably kind to her. Such favouritism.</p>

<p>Soyo: It wouldn't be surprising if Saki wanted to marry. Her father's just died; running a place this size alone must be difficult. When she marries, I intend to give her ten million yen.</p>

<p>Uika: If she marries, as someone of standing… naturally I'll use my connections to do something for her.</p>

<p>Soyo: Just come to the wedding empty-handed. Part of that ten million will be on your behalf. By then, I'm sure we'll be good friends.</p>

<p>Uika: Ha. I'll be the one sending the invitations. I don't advertise what I do for her like some nouveau riche… Hey, you over there! Come here.</p>

<p>Soyo: (Changing the subject again…)</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Yes, guest? What can I do for you?</p>

<p>Uika: Guest? You don't even know the proper form of address?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Sorry…</p>

<p>Soyo: Tell me, how is Saki?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: She's well, Sensei.</p>

<p>Uika: Is she up?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Yes, Sensei.</p>

<p>Uika: Idiot…</p>

<p>Mutsumi: …What is it, Sensei? Have I offended you?</p>

<p>Uika: Who told you to call me Sensei?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: But you don't enjoy that distinction alone. I've always used it for Ms Nagasaki, too.</p>

<p>Uika: Hmph… I'm different from her. How can you address us by the same title?</p>

<p>Soyo: Oh… Mutsumi Wakaba, can you tell the difference between us?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Certainly… from the daily bills.</p>

<p>Uika: Go and tell Sakiko to come here. I want to speak to her.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: With pleasure, Sensei… Sorry. I seem to have said it again.</p>

<p>Uika: Three months here and still like this. No manners.</p>

<p>Soyo: Want to see the difference between me and Dr Misumi?</p>

<p>Uika: What are you planning now?</p>

<p>Soyo: Mutsumi, here's ten thousand yen. Call Dr Misumi Sensei again and I'll give you another ten thousand.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: You're so generous! Thank you, Ms Nagasaki. And thank you, Sensei…</p>

<p>Uika: Get out…</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Thank you again, Master Nagasaki. I'll fetch the innkeeper.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Soyo: So very amusing, wouldn't you agree?</p>

<p>Uika: You think presents make you superior? My intellect is worth more than all your wealth.</p>

<p>Soyo: I don't care about what's valuable. I care about what I can spend.</p>

<p>Uika: Keep throwing money at her. She doesn't take you seriously.</p>

<p>Soyo: And you think she cares about your books? She needs money.</p>

<p>Uika: What money? She needs someone who can solve problems when it matters.</p>

<p>Soyo: Meaning someone who can help her borrow a hundred thousand yen?</p>

<p>Uika: Such insolence! Show some respect.</p>

<p>Soyo: If you had money, everyone would flatter you.</p>

<p>Uika: Absurd nonsense.</p>

<p>Soyo: Still better expressed than your ideas.</p>

<p>Tomori: Friends, what are you arguing about? What's the disagreement?</p>

<p>Soyo: She's upset by a brilliant observation of mine.</p>

<p>Uika: Master Nagasaki is preaching that education is useless again.</p>

<p>Soyo: I'm not particularly interested in qualifications, but I have nothing against knowledge. Still, it takes money to indulge life's little whims.</p>

<p>Tomori: Naturally, Master Nagasaki.</p>

<p>Uika: Enough! Let's talk about something else.</p>

<p>Tomori: How did you come to quarrel like this?</p>

<p>Soyo: For an amusing reason.</p>

<p>Uika: Ha. You turn everything into a joke.</p>

<p>Soyo: Our doctor has fallen in love with the innkeeper too, though my love far exceeds hers. She thinks her degree entitles her to the innkeeper's love. I merely hope for some return on my care. Isn't it funny?</p>

<p>Uika: Do you know how much I've done for her?</p>

<p>Soyo: You do things for her. I pay for things for her.</p>

<p>Tomori: I don't understand all this excitement. Losing your composure over feelings? Since finishing middle school, I've never quarrelled with anyone over such inexplicable emotions. I don't fall in love; I've never felt my heart stir. I've always considered irrational emotion extremely harmful.</p>

<p>Uika: But Sakiko has her own charm.</p>

<p>Soyo: I must agree with Dr Misumi there. Our innkeeper really is lovely.</p>

<p>Uika: I love her, so she must be special.</p>

<p>Tomori: Ridiculous. What's special about her? She seems no different from anyone else.</p>

<p>Uika: Her noble bearing captivated me.</p>

<p>Soyo: Her voice, her make-up, her face—like a rose.</p>

<p>Tomori: Where do you see that? I've stayed here three days without noticing anything special.</p>

<p>Soyo: You haven't looked at her properly.</p>

<p>Tomori: I see her every day. Apart from that blue hair feeling familiar, she's like anyone else.</p>

<p>Uika: Sakiko is different. She's… truly, truly special. I've never met anyone who combines bitterness and sweetness so perfectly.</p>

<p>Soyo: Unbelievable… I get on well with plenty of people and know exactly what they want. But I've spent so much on Saki without even holding her hand.</p>

<p>Tomori: Pathetic. I'd never waste my efforts like that.</p>

<p>Soyo: Have you never loved anyone?</p>

<p>Tomori: Not since the confusion of youth passed. Love, to me, is merely an illusion people invent.</p>

<p>Soyo: But aren't you an only child? Don't you want a child of your own?</p>

<p>Tomori: Of course. I've thought about it often. But if that requires living with someone else, I'd rather not.</p>

<p>Soyo: Then what's your wealth for?</p>

<p>Tomori: Enjoying life, naturally. Occasionally sharing its pleasures with friends like you.</p>

<p>Uika: Well said, Bachelor Takamatsu.</p>

<p>Soyo: What about love? Won't you be lonely?</p>

<p>Tomori: I can't afford it.</p>

<p>Soyo: Look! Sakiko! Our innkeeper.</p>

<p>Tomori: Pretty… but an Adélie penguin is much cuter to me.</p>

<p>Uika: Lovely again today…</p>

<p>Tomori: Never mind. I'd rather go to the roof and observe Venus.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Sakiko: What can I do for you all?</p>

<p>Uika: I want to ask you something, but not here.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Where would you like me to go, Uika?</p>

<p>Uika: Well… to my room.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Your room? If you need anything, I'll send a waiter.</p>

<p>Soyo: Dear Saki, I can say what I want in public. No room required. Look at these earrings. Do you like them?</p>

<p>Sakiko: They're beautiful.</p>

<p>Soyo: Diamonds.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Yes, I know. I meant the diamonds are beautiful.</p>

<p>Soyo: They're yours now.</p>

<p>Tomori: (Why throw money into the sea?)</p>

<p>Sakiko: Why would you give me these earrings?</p>

<p>Uika: A fine gift. Unfortunately, Sakiko already has a better pair.</p>

<p>Soyo: This is the latest style. Please accept them, darling.</p>

<p>Tomori: (Madness.)</p>

<p>Sakiko: I can't… they're too expensive…</p>

<p>Soyo: Refusing would be an insult.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Very well… I suppose I must accept, rather than spoil your pleasure.</p>

<p>Uika: Ha. A considerable gift indeed, Master Nagasaki. Merely to gratify your own vanity. Saki, our conversation should take place privately, looking into each other's eyes.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I'll come if nobody else needs anything.</p>

<p>Tomori: Innkeeper. The pattern on my sheets is offensive to my eyes. It doesn't suit my taste. Change them immediately or I'm checking out today.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I'll certainly find you better sheets, but please show a little courtesy when issuing commands.</p>

<p>Tomori: I've spent my money. I don't intend to spend my courtesy too.</p>

<p>Soyo: Forgive her. She's always been eccentric.</p>

<p>Tomori: I don't need her forgiveness.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Why treat me like this? What have I done wrong, Bachelor Takamatsu?</p>

<p>Tomori: Enough. Don't try that on me. Change the sheets. I'll send someone to check. Ladies, excuse me.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Sakiko: So rude! I've never met anyone like her.</p>

<p>Soyo: Don't be upset, Saki. Not everyone appreciates your charm.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I've had enough of her arrogance. I want to throw her out right now!</p>

<p>Uika: Say the word and I'll do it.</p>

<p>Soyo: I'll cover all the lost room charges. Better still, throw out the doctor beside me as well. I'll pay for that too.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Thank you both, but I'm only complaining. I can handle something this minor myself. My inn has never lacked guests.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Sensei, someone is asking for you.</p>

<p>Soyo: Who?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: A senior designer from a fashion company. (Quietly.) Saki, know when to stop.</p>

<p>Soyo: Ah yes, she's brought jewellery for me to see. Saki, I want something to match those earrings.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Really, there's no need… Soyo…</p>

<p>Soyo: These things suit you. It's nothing to me. I'll look at the jewellery now. Goodbye, Saki.</p>

<p>Uika: (That wretched master's graduate and her filthy money.)</p>

<p>Sakiko: Master Nagasaki really is spending too much.</p>

<p>Uika: That's what people with a little money are like. Showing off to gratify their vanity. I've seen plenty.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I understand.</p>

<p>Uika: They think a few presents will win over someone like you.</p>

<p>Sakiko: There's no harm in accepting gifts.</p>

<p>Uika: I'd be insulting you if I kept giving you things.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Naturally. You've never insulted me.</p>

<p>Uika: If there's anything I can do, just ask, Saki.</p>

<p>Sakiko: First I'd have to know what you can do.</p>

<p>Uika: Er…</p>

<p>Sakiko: Anyway, thank you, Uika.</p>

<p>Uika: I'm almost beginning to resent all those years of study.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Why?</p>

<p>Uika: Sometimes I wish I were like Master Nagasaki… (Quietly.) If only I'd decided to find you in Tokyo back then…</p>

<p>Sakiko: Because of her money?</p>

<p>Uika: That money is worthless. If I were a ridiculous person like her…</p>

<p>Sakiko: What would you do?</p>

<p>Uika: Enough! I'd propose to you!</p>

<p>Sakiko: What did she just say? That Dr Misumi wants to propose to me? Even if she does, there's one small problem. I don't want to give myself to her. I like triangular patterns… but Misumi herself? Far too abstract to be useful. I have no time to indulge her ponderous self-admiration.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Everyone who approaches me falls in love and fawns over me. Managing them every day is exhausting. Yet this Bachelor Takamatsu, cold as a penguin, treats me like that? She's the first person to dislike me so much. Of course, I'm not saying everyone has to like me. But what gives her the right to dismiss, with such unquestioning coldness, the dignity I work so hard to maintain?</p>

<p>Sakiko: She hates feelings? Hates love? Poor thing. She's simply afraid of connection and hides behind a shell of reason. But she'll encounter it. She will. Who knows? Perhaps she already has. Now I'm curious about this Bachelor Takamatsu. I want to see whether she can stay so cold.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Knowledge doesn't interest me. Wealth does, and doesn't. What I enjoy most… is watching people serve me, adore me, lose their heads over me, fall under my control. Marriage? Never considered it. I need nobody. If I wanted marriage, why didn't I return to the Togawa family? I live respectably by myself and enjoy my freedom. I can grant them a moment's dream, but I'll never hand over my heart.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I'm going to have some fun with them. I'll use every means to tear apart that supposedly invincible heart… Since fate has put me here, you can all perform your parts in my play.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Mutsumi: Saki…</p>

<p>Sakiko: What is it?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: A guest in the corridor wants fresh sheets.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I know. She told me too.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Leave them by the door. I'll take them over.</p>

<p>Sakiko: No need. I'll take them myself.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Saki? Yourself?</p>

<p>Sakiko: Yes.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: You seem to care a great deal about this guest.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Every guest matters. Do your own job.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: (Saki keeps stringing me along…)</p>

<p>Sakiko: (I ought to give Mutsumi a little sweetness, or why would she willingly stay?)</p>

<p>Mutsumi: I've always served the guests.</p>

<p>Sakiko: You're too cold with them.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: You're too kind to them.</p>

<p>Sakiko: I know what I'm doing. I don't need your advice.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: … Find another waiter.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Mutsumi? Have you come to hate me?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Saki, do you remember what Uncle Kiyotsugu told us before he died?</p>

<p>Sakiko: Of course. I remember every promise I made.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: I don't want you to serve her.</p>

<p>Sakiko: What sort of person do you think I am? Stupid? Frivolous? Licentious? Do you think I enjoy running around for people, forcing a smile? This is for our interests and the inn's reputation. I don't depend on those presents. As for a lover, one is enough, and I already have that person. When I want to marry… naturally, I remember Father's words… Whoever has sincerely cared for me won't be disappointed in future. I understand gratitude and devotion. Unfortunately, nobody understands me… If only you could, Mutsumi.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: I can't work you out, Saki. One moment you seem to like me, the next you don't. I love you. I want you to live a little better. That's why I work here… Perhaps I really should turn a blind eye. Fortunately, guests come and go. I'm the only one who can remain beside her.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Mutsumi: Sensei, your letter.</p>

<p>Tomori: An Earl Grey, please. Your best leaves.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Certainly. One moment.</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Tomori opens the letter and reads aloud.)</p>

<p>Tomori: Tokyo, April 2030. Dear Tomori Takamatsu, I'm the arranger who worked with you last time. Is there such a person? I don't remember. Our profound friendship prompts me to bring you good news. Interesting. I understand you're twenty-six and still single, so I'd like to introduce my cousin, Mana Sumita. She's a five-time champion of the National Amateur Singing Contest and has inherited a considerable fortune. I hope you might…</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Tomori tears up the letter.)</p>

<p>Tomori: Spare yourself the trouble. What do her inheritance and titles have to do with me? Living alone, I can manage with less money. With a partner, no amount would suffice. I'd rather be a smelly penguin all my life than marry.</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Uika appears.)</p>

<p>Uika: May I sit here?</p>

<p>Tomori: Of course. Sit down.</p>

<p>Uika: At least we can speak openly to one another… How could that worthless master's graduate join our conversation?</p>

<p>Tomori: I only have a bachelor's degree.</p>

<p>Uika: Better than a master's earned in a year.</p>

<p>Tomori: Show others some respect, Dr Misumi.</p>

<p>Uika: You know me. I'm courteous to everyone. She's the one exception I can't endure.</p>

<p>Tomori: You quarrel because you love the same person. People like us should keep our distance from someone like her. She's not worth it.</p>

<p>Uika: No… Saki's charm is incomparable.</p>

<p>Tomori: Hopeless. The same tricks every time: sweet talk and showing herself off, and you're bewitched? Why are you so obsessed with love anyway? Lust? No wonder you still haven't published in one of the top four journals.</p>

<p>Uika: Stop! All right, I do care about her… But what really troubles me is that there's still no news about the paper I submitted months ago.</p>

<p>Tomori: …</p>

<p>Uika: Effort can change so little…</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Mutsumi places an Earl Grey before them.)</p>

<p>Tomori: Sorry, I forgot to order you one… Bring this guest another Earl Grey like mine.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: We're out of those leaves today.</p>

<p>Tomori: If you don't mind, this cup…</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Uika picks it up and starts drinking.)</p>

<p>Tomori: Er… (She actually took it… What do I drink?)</p>

<p>Uika: I compiled a textbook recently. The publisher promised… (Sips tea.) …two million yen in royalties. (Sips again.)</p>

<p>Tomori: That's very good.</p>

<p>Uika: But the agreed date has passed… (Sips.) …and they haven't paid. So the problem is… (Sips.) The problem is… (Finishes the tea.) I'm a little short just now, so…</p>

<p>Tomori: Well… perhaps the money will arrive in a few days.</p>

<p>Uika: You know how much people like us value our word. I need money urgently…</p>

<p>Tomori: Ah… you have my sympathy… (Oh no. How do I escape this?)</p>

<p>Uika: Would you help me with this small favour?</p>

<p>Tomori: Ahaha… Uika, I'd gladly help if I had the money… I'm waiting for royalties too.</p>

<p>Uika: You can't fool me. You just ordered that tea. That cup must cost quite a bit.</p>

<p>Tomori: All right. This is what I have on me.</p>

<p class="script-direction">(She opens her wallet: a hundred thousand yen and some coins.)</p>

<p>Uika: A hundred thousand.</p>

<p>Tomori: It's the last money I have…</p>

<p>Uika: Then lend me the hundred thousand.</p>

<p>Tomori: What am I supposed to do afterwards?</p>

<p>Uika: Don't worry. I'll repay you.</p>

<p>Tomori: Fine…</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Tomori hands Uika the money.)</p>

<p>Uika: I'll be off. See you at lunch.</p>

<p>Tomori: … All right… First Dr Misumi calmly boasts about two million in royalties; then she delights in my hundred thousand. Better if she never repays me. At least she won't bother me again. It's only a small sum anyway.</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Tomori notices the empty cup.)</p>

<p>Tomori: And the tea hasn't even been paid for… How rude.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Sakiko: Tomori, may I come in?</p>

<p>Tomori: Tomori? What do you want?</p>

<p>Sakiko: I've brought you better sheets.</p>

<p>Tomori: Leave them on that table.</p>

<p>Sakiko: You'll certainly like this pattern.</p>

<p>Tomori: What is it?</p>

<p>Sakiko: Limited-edition penguin sheets from the nearby aquarium.</p>

<p>Tomori: Nothing so expensive was necessary. A little better would have done.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Oh, and I found a piece of marble nearby. Look.</p>

<p>Tomori: Enough of this flattery. Where did you find out about me?</p>

<p>Sakiko: (Still on her guard.) Tomori, what would you like for lunch?</p>

<p>Tomori: Anything.</p>

<p>Sakiko: But I'd like to know your tastes, so I can prepare it.</p>

<p>Tomori: I'll tell the waiter.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Mutsumi isn't as attentive as I am in these matters. If you'd like something special, just say.</p>

<p>Tomori: Thank you, but don't try handling me the way you handle those two.</p>

<p>Sakiko: They're simply too quick to fall in love. I'm only doing business. Agreeing with them is just a way to keep my guests.</p>

<p>Tomori: Honest enough.</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Sakiko puts down the sheets and picks up a notebook.)</p>

<p>Sakiko: This notebook… how nostalgic. I used this series as a child.</p>

<p>Tomori: Put it down. Going through other people's things is rude.</p>

<p>Sakiko: Wanting… to become a human's song? Did you write these? “Although I've made friends like everyone else…” “Even though I'm with everyone…” Are these lyrics?</p>

<p>Tomori: Yes…</p>

<p>Tomori: (Why bring me here?) Where are we?</p>

<p>Sakiko: My piano room. I used to love music, but I had to give it up. Only last year did I save enough to buy a piano again. To make up for that old regret, I set aside a room in the inn for it. I may be rusty.</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Sakiko plays and sings the lyrics Tomori wrote long, long ago.)</p>

<p>Tomori: (So it was you… The girl who knocked me down on the bridge.)</p>

<p>Sakiko: Thank you for listening. I love music. I once wanted to form a band, but…</p>

<p>Tomori: (If only I'd found the courage to invite her home then… What am I thinking?)</p>

<p>Sakiko: Give me your hand.</p>

<p>Tomori: What are you doing?</p>

<p class="script-direction">(Sakiko takes her hand.)</p>

<p>Sakiko: See? Now you can feel my warmth. People aren't so hard to understand. We're connected to one another. Sorry to have taken so much of your time. I must return to work. It feels good to talk freely, without flirtation or entanglements.</p>

<p>Tomori: You're… leaving already? (What are you doing? I don't understand.) Go ahead…</p>

<p>Sakiko: All right.</p>

<p>Tomori: If you have time… you could come and sit with me…</p>

<p>Sakiko: I don't usually visit guests' rooms. But you're an exception. Because… I like you very much.</p>

<p>Tomori: What?! You like me?</p>

<p>Sakiko: Your lyrics feel like a cry from your heart. And you're the only person who doesn't stare at me strangely. I feel safe with you. (Just as planned.) (After all that, if she hasn't fallen for me by tomorrow, I'll eat this piano.)</p>

<p>Tomori: …I wish she'd stay away. But… she likes my earliest lyrics. No, don't go… She really is special. We could be friends… Yes. Friends. I won't fall in love with her.</p>

<hr class="script-scene-break">

<p>Mutsumi: Um… honoured guests? Your rooms are on the third floor. The private dining room is on the left; the VIP lounge is on the right.</p>

<p>Nyamu: Understood… Are you an employee or the owner?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: An employee. Call me Mutsumi. At your service whenever you need me.</p>

<p>Raana: (Honoured guests?)</p>

<p>Nyamu: Mutsuko?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Yes?</p>

<p>Nyamu: Have your innkeeper come here. We'd like to speak to her.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: I'll tell her. (She carries herself like Master Nagasaki. Another society lady, presumably.)</p>

<p>Raana: She seems fooled. She really believes you're rich.</p>

<p>Nyamu: Away from home, you give yourself your own identity. Then people treat you properly.</p>

<p>Raana: Won't that cost more?</p>

<p>Nyamu: Relax. I'll get us a discount.</p>

<p>Raana: Could there be trouble?</p>

<p>Nyamu: I play society ladies, princesses and jealous wives on camera every day. How could they see through me? I'm sick of plum rice on set. Look, even the restaurant has private rooms. Don't you want a matcha parfait?</p>

<p>Raana: Matcha parfait… It's been so long… But when the rest of the troupe arrives, we'll be exposed…</p>

<p>Nyamu: They won't reach Tokyo today. That's what they get for taking the coach.</p>

<p>Raana: They have no money.</p>

<p>Nyamu: We're lucky we got an economy ticket.</p>

<p>Raana: Good thing we played that live show.</p>

<p>Nyamu: My idea. Eat whatever you want in the restaurant.</p>

<p>Raana: Really?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Don't know.</p>

<p>Tomori: That wicked woman! I'll catch her.</p>

<p>Tomori: What do you know? None of your business!</p>

<p>Mutsumi: You want to drag her to your room.</p>

<p>Tomori: Move!</p>

<p>Mutsumi: No.</p>

<p>Tomori: I heard your conversation. That wicked woman won't answer me, but whispers with you in her room.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: What are you talking about?</p>

<p>…</p>

<p>Tomori: What's that sound?</p>

<p>Sakiko: (Quietly.) Isn't that…</p>

<p>Tomori: Who? Who's playing my 『Haruhikage』? SOYO NAGASAKI!</p>

<p>Mutsumi: I see.</p>

<p>Sakiko: (Under someone else's roof. Completely at her mercy.) Um… You know my situation. You help run the inn, too.</p>

<p>Mutsumi: I've never been happy there. Serving tea, cleaning—I hate those jobs. But you needed me, so I stayed. Yet you're always watching somebody else… Uika, Soyo, Tomori… How much do I matter to you?</p>

<p>Mutsumi: Haven't you always ordered me about like a servant? Like a dog, wagging its tail, coming and going at your call?</p>
