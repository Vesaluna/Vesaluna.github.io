This is a reading guide, not a reproduction of the original or its translations. Kunihiko Kodaira describes the slow work of reading mathematics: examining a theorem, trying to prove it, returning to the printed proof, copying and questioning it, and sometimes even rearranging a whole chapter. The question is not how many pages one has “read,” but when understanding begins. My archive also holds Chinese and English translations, which I have not copied here.

Author: Kunihiko Kodaira. The original appeared in the August 1980 issue of *Suugaku Seminar*. My archived English text attributes its translation to Florian Sprung. An [online English version](https://www.zhihu.com/en/article/683420720) is available, but it is not the original journal site; I could not verify a stable page for the original publication. The date here records when I created my local note.
